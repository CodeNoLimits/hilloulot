Life

Rabbi Menashe Klein, the Admor of Ungvar, was born on 5 Nissan 5683 (1923) in Irlyava, near Ungvar, the son of Rabbi Eliezer Zeev, rabbi of the town and a student of the Minchat Elazar, and of Rachel Leah. Rabbi Chaim Elazar Shapira of Munkatch, his mohel, asked that he be named Menashe. In 5692 he studied with Rabbi Yossef Elimelech Kahana of Ungvar, who appointed him to give lessons at the yeshiva. Deported in the Shoah, he lost his family, survived Auschwitz, Buna and Buchenwald and was liberated on 28 Nissan 5705. After a year in France he reached the United States, headed the Beit Shearim yeshiva in Brooklyn, then opened Ungvar Hasidism in Israel. He died on 29 Elul 5771.

Works

He is known for the responsa series Mishneh Halakhot and wrote Maggid Mishneh on the Torah (five parts), Mitzvat HaMelech, Mal VeLo Para, Um Ani Chomah and Yemei Shemoneh, as well as Pirsumei Nissa, an autobiographical account, questions and answers and elegies on the Shoah, written on the ship to France.
