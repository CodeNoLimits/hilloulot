Life

Rabbi Tzvi Pesach Frank was born in 1873 in Kovno, Lithuania, to Malka and Rabbi Yehuda Leib Frank, a leader of the local Hovevei Zion association. He studied at the Slobodka and Telz yeshivot, where the rosh yeshiva, Rabbi Eliezer Gordon, showed him particular regard. He went up to the Land of Israel in 1893, shortly before his parents, who were among the founders of the colony of Hadera, where his father was the first rabbi. A judge and then president of the rabbinical court of Jerusalem, a leading halakhic authority, a co-founder of the Chief Rabbinate and a member of its Council, he served as Ashkenazi Chief Rabbi of Jerusalem from 1936 to 1960. He died on 21 Kislev 5721 (10 December 1960) and rests on Har HaMenuchot.

Works

His work is the series “Har Tzvi”, which includes eight volumes of responsa (on the four parts of the Shulchan Aruch and on Zeraim), a commentary on the Talmud, notes on the Shulchan Aruch and on the Rambam’s Mishneh Torah, and commentaries on the Torah. He also wrote “Shvivei Or”, “Mikraei Kodesh” (on the festivals), “Hadrat Kodesh” (on the life cycle), “Mikdash Melech” (on the laws of the Temple and the Temple Mount) and “Esa Einai el HeHarim” (letters of guidance and ethics for Torah study and fear of Heaven).
