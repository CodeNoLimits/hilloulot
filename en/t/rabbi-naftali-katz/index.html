Life

Rabbi Naftali Katz was born around 1648 in Ostroh, today in Ukraine, to Rabbi Yitzchak HaCohen, a descendant of the Maharal of Prague, and Leidel. In his youth he was taken captive by the Tatars but managed to escape. He married Esther Shindel, daughter of the av beit din of Ostroh, who appointed him rosh yeshiva and gabbai of the beit midrash. He later served as head of the chevra kadisha and av beit din of Posen, then settled in Frankfurt, where he deepened his study of hidden wisdom. In 1711 a fire that started in Frankfurt’s Jewish quarter was blamed on him; he was arrested and then acquitted, but had to leave the city. He then went to Prague, where he served as rav in the yeshiva of Rabbi David Oppenheim. He died on 24 Tevet 5479 (15 January 1719) in Constantinople.

Works

His book Semichat Chachamim explains the order of the tractates in relation to one another; his name has been remembered by this title. He also wrote Birkat HaShem (which includes Semichat Chachamim and Kedusha VeBracha, novellae on tractate Berachot), Pi Yesharim, Sha’ar HaHachana, Sha’ar Naftali, Meshech HaZera (a commentary on the order Zeraim) and Beit Rachel (a siddur).
