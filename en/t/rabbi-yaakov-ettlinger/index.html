Life

Rabbi Yaakov Ettlinger was born in Karlsruhe on 29 Adar 5558 (17 March 1798), the fourth of eleven children of Rabbi Aharon and Rachel. His father, a synagogue rabbi, was his first teacher; he then studied with the city rabbi, Rabbi Asher, and afterwards at the yeshiva of Rabbi Avraham Bing, av beit din of Würzburg, where he also attended the university. After the “Hep-Hep” riots of 1819 he took refuge in Fürth. Appointed in 1826 rabbi of a district and settled in Mannheim, he headed the Klaus and founded a yeshiva that trained rabbis, among them Rabbi Samson Raphael Hirsch. In 1836 he became chief rabbi of Altona, a post he held until the end of his life, and founded a yeshiva there too, where Rabbi Azriel Hildesheimer studied among others. In 1844, after the Reform rabbis’ conference in Brunswick, he led a protest of one hundred and sixteen rabbis. He died in Altona on 25 Kislev 5632 (7 December 1871).

Works

His major work “Aruch LaNer”, a commentary on the Babylonian Talmud (tractates Rosh Hashana, Sukkah, Yevamot, Sanhedrin, Makkot, Keritot and Niddah), appeared from 1850 to 1873; he gave it that name to perpetuate his parents’ names. He also wrote “Bikurei Yaakov” (laws of Sukkah, 1836), the responsa “Binyan Tziyon” (1868, then “HaChadashot”, 1873), “Minchat Ani” (sermons on the Torah, 1874) and “Shomer Tziyon HaNe’eman”.
