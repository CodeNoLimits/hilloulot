Life

Rabbi Yitzchak Yosef Zilber was born in Kazan in 1917, son of Rabbi Ben-Zion Chaim Zilber, the city’s rabbi. His father did not send him to school, to avoid desecrating Shabbat, and taught him the secular subjects himself, along with Bible, Mishnah and Talmud. Strictly observant, he was dismissed from work, questioned, and sentenced in 1951 to two years in a labor camp. He went up to Israel in 1972 and founded the “Shvi Golah” yeshiva and the “Toldot Yeshurun” association for immigrants from Russia. He died on the eve of Tisha B’Av 5764 (2004).

Works

He wrote “VeLahava Lo Tiv’ar Bach” (on the fulfilment of the prophecies, in Russian in 1984, in Hebrew in 2002), “Sichot al HaTorah” (2000, in Russian) and “Lehisha’er Yehudi” (2004, in Russian).
